How to play

  1. Pick a clue. The player in control chooses a category and value. Whoever wins a clue takes control.
  2. Typed responses (no buzzers). The clue appears and you both type a response at once, with 30 seconds on the clock. The closest answer wins the clue's value. If you're equally close, you both get it. If neither answer is close, nobody scores, and nobody ever loses money on a regular clue.
  3. How "close" is measured. Each response is scored by how many of the correct response's key words it contains, so spelling counts a little. Wording that matches the flashcard scores highest; other accepted answers (like "Israel" for "the northern kingdom of Israel") top out at 90%. You don't need "What is". If the check gets one wrong, either of you can overrule it once the correct response is shown.
  4. Out-loud responses (buzzers). Buzzers open when the lights beside the clue come on; buzz too early and you're locked out for a quarter second. On two devices press Space or tap BUZZ; on one screen player 1 presses A and player 2 presses L. Wrong answers cost the clue's value.
  5. Daily Doubles. Hidden behind one clue in Round One and two in the Double Round. Only the player who finds it answers, after wagering up to their score or the round's top value. A wrong answer loses the wager.
  6. Final Round. Anyone with more than $0 wagers in secret, sees the clue, and has 45 seconds to write a response.
  7. Covering the deck. Each new game deals the categories you've seen least, so four full games cover all 167 flashcards. Clues nobody gets go on your study list.
